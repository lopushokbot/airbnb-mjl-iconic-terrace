How pricing works now — one number, two sides

The listing migrated to Airbnb's 15.5% host-only service fee in August 2026 (the co-host's pricing tool raised posted rates ×1.155 to compensate). There is no separate guest service fee anymore: the nightly price you post is exactly what the guest sees, and you net posted × 0.845. The old "guest sees ≈ ×1.22" rule is dead.

Worked example — 3 nights in November at posted AED 960: the guest sees AED 960/night (AED 2,880 accommodation), plus the cleaning fee AED 140, Tourism Dirham AED 45 (15 × 3 nights) and 5% VAT as separate line items. You net AED 811/night (960 × 0.845).

Cleaning fee: AED 140 now → 200 after review #15. Tourism Dirham: AED 15/bedroom/night (holiday-home "Deluxe" rate — the AED 20 figure is the 5-star-hotel rate). 5% UAE VAT rides on top as its own line. All three stay outside the nightly rate.

This is still a review-velocity year, not a revenue year. The first 5-star review has landed — but with 1 review, the listing keeps pricing ~15% below the Madinat comp median to win bookings. Every early 5-star review is worth ~AED 40–60/night in future pricing power, so the strategy remains: fill the calendar, harvest reviews.

The edge — space & terrace, not a view

This unit has no view (ground floor, courtyard-facing). Don't compete on view with Marina / JBR / Port de la Mer listings — you'll lose. Compete on what's genuinely scarce in MJL: a 91.5 m² floor plan with a large private hedged terrace and direct pool access, in Dubai's best family-tourism location.

MJL puts guests within a 5-minute walk of Burj Al Arab, Souk Madinat Jumeirah, Wild Wadi and the Madinat waterways — it is the family-and-couples base in Dubai. The terrace is this listing's "view equivalent": photograph it at golden hour, set for outdoor dining, and lead every listing photo with it.

Address the ground-floor objection head-on in the copy: "private hedged terrace, no through-traffic, steps from the pool." Turn the perceived negative into the headline feature.
